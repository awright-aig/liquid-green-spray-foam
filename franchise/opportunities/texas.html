Why Texas Is a Strong Franchise Market

Texas is the second-largest state in the U.S. by both population and land area, with approximately 30 million residents spread across a high-growth, pro-business economy. The state runs on its own power grid — the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) — has no state income tax, and consistently ranks at the top nationally for new construction starts. For a spray foam franchisee, the math is straightforward: more houses means more insulation work, and Texas builds more houses than anywhere else in America.

The four major metros — Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, Austin, and San Antonio — each represent multi-county franchise territories on their own. Layer them together and you have a statewide opportunity that few franchise systems have the operating model to cover. The hot Texas climate is a structural advantage for the product: spray foam delivers some of the highest R-value per inch of any residential insulation, which translates directly into lower cooling load — the line item that decides whether a Texas summer electric bill is manageable or brutal. With most of the state sitting in IECC climate zones 2A through 3B, the energy code pulls in the same direction: tighter envelopes, higher R-values, less air infiltration.

Construction Industry & New Build

Construction is one of the largest industries in Texas, and the state leads the country in single-family housing permits year after year. Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, and San Antonio all sit in the top ten U.S. metros for housing starts, and the pace of new residential construction has stayed strong even as other markets have cooled. For a spray foam operator, that is the foundation of the work: builders specify insulation in the plans, and open-cell and closed-cell foam are increasingly the products of choice for high-performance Texas homes.

New build is the heart of the Texas opportunity. Energy code adoption in Texas follows the International Energy Conservation Code (IECC), and most major Texas cities have adopted recent versions, which lifts the baseline for insulation performance. The result is more code-driven demand for high-R-value insulation in new construction, not just retrofits. Commercial and light-industrial work — warehouses, retail, self-storage, agricultural outbuildings — adds a second channel, and the metro growth corridors (north of Austin, north of DFW, west of Houston) keep feeding the pipeline. Treat the build pipeline as the indicator to watch; volume can shift market to market.

Energy Efficiency Tailwind

Texas has unique energy dynamics, and they cut directly in favor of spray foam. The state's isolated ERCOT grid was tested brutally during Winter Storm Uri in February 2021, when millions of customers lost power for days in a deep freeze, and again during Hurricane Beryl in July 2024. Both events pushed energy efficiency — and home hardening — to the top of the homeowner priority list. The conversation on a Texas job site is no longer "should I add insulation?" — it is "how much and where?"

Cooling load is the second tailwind, and it is year-round. Texas summers push air-conditioning systems to their limits, and spray foam insulation seals the building envelope, reduces air infiltration, and cuts the cooling load on the HVAC equipment. For a homeowner in Houston, Austin, or San Antonio, the energy savings on a foam-insulated attic or wall assembly are real and measurable, and they show up on the very next electric bill. The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25E) allows homeowners to claim a portion of qualifying insulation expenses, which helps close the sale on retrofit jobs. Plan retrofit pricing around ranges, not promises; actual customer savings vary by home, HVAC system, and usage.

Local Work & Jobs Across the State

A Texas Liquid Green franchise is a local business. The rig is mobile, the customers are local, and the crew is hired locally. The statewide development right is structured for operators who want to build a regional platform — multiple territories, multiple crews, multiple metros — but the work itself is hometown work, job by job. Every installation is in a protected territory; no other Liquid Green operator is bidding on the same builder down the street.

Job creation is direct. A single spray foam rig typically supports a foreman and two to three installers, with growth into dispatch, sales, and office staff as the territory matures. A statewide development scales that to multiple crews and an operations backbone. The economic footprint is real local dollars — payroll, vehicle fuel, materials purchased from regional suppliers, and contracts with local builders and remodelers. Texas cities have tight contractor networks — builder associations, remodeling councils, REI chapters — where a new operator can plug in and start generating referrals within weeks. The Liquid Green brand and operating system give a new operator a head start in those networks; the work and the relationships are local. Treat job counts as a range; actual hiring varies by operator and market conditions.
