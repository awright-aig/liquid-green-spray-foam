Why New York Is a Strong Franchise Market

New York is the fourth-largest state in the country by population, with approximately 19.6 million residents spread across multiple distinct metros. New York City anchors the southeast, the Hudson Valley and Westchester stretch north, Long Island covers Nassau and Suffolk, the Capital Region around Albany runs up the Hudson, and the I-90 corridor ties together Syracuse, Rochester, and Buffalo. Each of these metros is its own market with its own builder networks, code officials, utility rebates, and homeowner base.

That diversity is the reason a statewide operator is the right fit. A single-territory franchise can own one county and run it well. A statewide development partner owns the whole map and builds the sub-territories under one playbook. The unit economics work because a spray foam rig is mobile — one operator with the right scheduling can run jobs from Yonkers to Syracuse in the same week without owning a second shop.

New York also has some of the most aggressive climate and energy policy in the country. The 2019 Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (CLCPA) sets a 70% renewable electricity target by 2030 and economy-wide carbon neutrality by 2050. The state's NYStretch energy code (an above-code stretch code that localities can adopt) raises the bar on envelope airtightness and R-value. NYSERDA runs the Empower Plus, Home Performance with ENERGY STAR, and NY-Sun programs that pay real dollars to homeowners and building owners for energy efficiency upgrades. The combination of policy and rebates turns "you should insulate" into "here is a check to insulate" — and that is the demand an operator runs on.

Construction Industry & New Build

New York is one of the largest construction markets in the country. Statewide construction spending runs in the tens of billions annually, split across residential, commercial, institutional, and infrastructure categories. NYC alone sees tens of thousands of new building permits each year, and the five boroughs run continuous work on housing, schools, healthcare, and commercial mixed-use. Upstate, Albany, Syracuse, Rochester, and Buffalo each have their own pipeline of public and private projects.

For a spray foam operator, the new construction side is straightforward. Builders need insulation subcontractors they can count on, with crews that show up, hit the schedule, and pass inspection. The builder relationship is a long game: one good general contractor in a hot market can keep a rig busy for a full quarter. The statewide development package supports operator-led builder outreach, with sales templates, marketing collateral, and a brand reputation that opens the door.

Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley, and the Capital Region are where the residential new build and addition market is most consistent. Upstate metros are where institutional and light commercial projects — schools, healthcare facilities, warehouses, retail — provide steady commercial work. A statewide operator develops sub-franchisees for each of these markets and builds a regional platform one territory at a time.

Energy Efficiency Tailwind

New York is not a state where energy efficiency is optional. NYSERDA administers rebate and incentive programs that put real money into the hands of homeowners and building owners who upgrade their envelope. The Home Performance with ENERGY STAR program pays for whole-home weatherization, including insulation and air sealing. Comfort Homes pays for similar work through participating contractors. NY-Sun pairs rooftop solar with envelope upgrades on a regular basis. Each of these programs creates paying work for a spray foam operator with the right certifications and the right paperwork process.

On the federal side, the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25C) and the Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) are both live and both apply to qualifying insulation work. Homeowners can claim 30% of qualifying insulation costs up to a capped annual amount. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed 2025) extended Section 25C through 2032 with an annual $3,200 cap and a $1,200 sub-limit for envelope work. The takeaway: federal tax credits are alive, generous, and pushing retrofit demand harder every year.

Codes do the same work from the new construction side. NYStretch is the most aggressive residential stretch code in the country, and localities across the state are adopting it. ASHRAE 90.1-2016 and the 2020 IECC are the baseline for commercial work. The R-value requirements, the air-sealing requirements, and the continuous-barrier requirements all point at spray foam as a high-performance solution. This is the structural tailwind the franchise model is built on.

Local Work & Jobs Across the State

A statewide development deal is bigger than a single-territory franchise, but the work is the same. Local work. Every spray foam job is performed in a neighborhood, on a building, by a local crew. The statewide operator hires spray foam technicians, crew leads, estimators, and operations staff. As sub-territories open, the operator recruits and trains sub-franchisees, with each new sub-territory bringing its own local hiring.

The job creation is real. Spray foam technicians are skilled trades positions that pay above the local average for entry-level work, with on-the-job training through the Liquid Green Spray Academy. The roles are full-time, year-round, and stable — they are not seasonal gig work. A statewide development typically creates dozens of local jobs over the first three to five years, with continued growth as new sub-territories are added. These are local jobs that stay in the community and support local families.

The community angle matters. Operators are hiring in their hometown, training in their hometown, and serving in their hometown. The homeowners and builders who hire the franchise are neighbors, not strangers. Referrals happen on the jobsite and at the school pickup line. That is the small-business advantage that a national installer cannot match, and it is the part of the model that operators consistently say they like best.

The statewide development agreement grants the master franchisee the right to develop and operate Liquid Green territories across New York State — NYC, Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley, the Capital Region (Albany), Syracuse, Rochester, and Buffalo. What's the construction market like in New York?

New York's construction market is one of the largest in the country. Approximately 19.6 million residents live across multiple metros — NYC, Long Island, Westchester, the Hudson Valley, Albany, Syracuse, Rochester, Buffalo — and the housing stock includes a significant share of older homes and pre-war buildings that are prime retrofit candidates. The demand mix is new construction plus retrofit, with retrofit growing fastest.
