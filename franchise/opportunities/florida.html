Why Florida Is a Strong Franchise Market

Florida is the third-largest state in the U.S. by population, with approximately 22 million residents, and it sits entirely in IECC climate zones 1A through 2A — the hot-humid band. That singular climate reality defines the work: air-conditioning runs ten months a year, humidity is structural, and the building envelope has to perform under relentless thermal and moisture load. Spray foam is one of the few insulation products engineered to handle all three at once. For a franchise operator, that is the technical edge in nearly every Florida sales conversation.

The market depth is the other half of the story. Florida is a year-round install window — there is no winter slowdown. The major metros — Miami, Tampa, Orlando, Jacksonville, Fort Myers, Naples — each support a multi-county territory on their own, and the Gulf Coast rebuild cycle after Hurricane Ian (2022) and Hurricane Milton (2024) is still feeding the work pipeline. Layer the metros together and you have a statewide opportunity that aligns cleanly with the Liquid Green multi-unit development model: a regional platform, a mobile rig fleet, and a long list of homeowner, builder, and commercial customers in every direction.

Construction Industry & New Build

Florida's construction industry is one of the largest in the country. The state has been a top-three U.S. market for single-family housing permits in most years over the past decade, and the multifamily and light-commercial pipelines are equally active. Population growth — domestic in-migration, plus a strong international inflow into South Florida — keeps the demand curve in front of supply.

What separates Florida from most other big states is the building code. The 2023 Florida Building Code, 7th Edition, is the strictest statewide wind-resistance code in the United States. It was adopted after Hurricane Ian's catastrophic damage in 2022 and tightened further after Hurricane Milton in 2024. The code raises the bar on roof-deck attachment, wall sheathing, opening protection, and continuous insulation. Closed-cell spray foam is one of the few insulation products that contributes to both the R-value requirement and the structural-rigidity requirement — it adheres to the substrate, hardens into a monolithic layer, and adds racking strength to wall and roof assemblies. That puts closed-cell foam in the spec on a meaningful share of new Florida construction, not just retrofits. New build is the heart of the opportunity, but the rebuild and retrofit channel — homes that took damage, homes that need to harden before the next storm — is just as active.

Energy Efficiency Tailwind

Florida's energy bill is one of the highest per-capita in the country, and the state has some of the most aggressive energy codes in the southeast. The 2023 Florida Building Code lifted the insulation baseline for new construction across IECC zones 1A and 2A, and the HVHZ (High-Velocity Hurricane Zone) in Miami-Dade and Broward carries additional envelope requirements on top of the state floor. The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25E) lets homeowners claim a portion of qualifying insulation expenses, which helps close the sale on retrofit work — particularly for homeowners who already saw one bad hurricane season's electric bill.

The practical effect is that energy efficiency is no longer a "nice to have" in Florida — it is built into the permit. A builder specifying to current code is already pulling foam into the wall and roof assembly, and a homeowner retrofitting an older home is buying a measurable reduction in cooling load, humidity intrusion, and noise. The conversation on the sales call has shifted. The pitch is no longer "why insulate?" — it is "which foam, where, and how much." Plan retrofit pricing around ranges; actual customer savings vary by home, HVAC system, and how the building is used.

Local Work & Jobs Across the State

A Florida Liquid Green franchise is a local business. The rig is mobile, the customers are local, and the crew is hired locally. The statewide development right is structured for operators who want to build a regional platform — multiple territories, multiple crews, multiple metros — but the work itself is hometown work, one job at a time. Every installation is in a protected territory; no other Liquid Green operator is bidding on the same builder down the street.

Job creation is direct. A single spray foam rig typically supports a foreman and two to three installers, with growth into dispatch, sales, and office staff as the territory matures. A statewide Florida development scales that to multiple crews and an operations backbone spread across the major metros. The economic footprint is real local dollars — payroll, vehicle fuel, materials purchased from regional suppliers, and contracts with local builders and remodelers. Florida's contractor networks — local HBA chapters, remodeling councils, REI groups — are well-organized and referral-friendly, which gives a new operator a faster path to the first ten jobs than a colder market would. The Liquid Green brand and operating system give the new operator a head start; the work and the relationships are local. Treat job counts as a range; actual hiring varies by operator and market conditions.

Statewide development rights cover Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach; the Tampa Bay and Southwest Florida region (Tampa, St. Petersburg, Sarasota, Fort Myers, Naples); the Orlando metro; and Northeast Florida (Jacksonville, St. Augustine).
