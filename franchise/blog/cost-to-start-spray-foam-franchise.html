How Much Does It Cost to Start a Spray Foam Franchise?

Cost, Profit & ROI Breakdown

By Harvey Barnes September 8, 2026 6 min read

Thinking about getting into spray foam? Before you buy a truck or sign anything, you want one number: what it really costs to get started. This guide breaks down the cost to start a spray foam business, what your money buys, and how much you might make.

What Does It Cost to Start a Spray Foam Franchise?

A spray foam franchise usually costs somewhere in the low-to-mid six figures to launch. With Liquid Green, the total startup investment ranges from $136,000 to $194,000, and that number covers your equipment, training, initial inventory, and launch support.

A franchise simply means you run your own local business using a proven brand, system, and playbook instead of building everything from scratch. You own the business, but you are not figuring it out alone.

That price also sits well below many big insulation brands. You can see the full picture on the franchise opportunity page.

What a Spray Foam Business Actually Does

Spray foam is a liquid that expands into a foam and hardens in place. A spray foam business sprays it into walls, attics, basements, and crawl spaces to seal air leaks and insulate a building in one step.

There are two main types, and most jobs use one or both:

  • Open cell foam — softer and lighter, great for interior walls and sound control.
  • Closed cell foam — denser and stronger, with a higher R-value and moisture resistance.

Franchise vs. Starting Your Own Spray Foam Business

You have two paths: start your own independent business or buy a franchise. The costs are closer than most people expect once you add everything up.

Going independent is not cheap. One equipment supplier reports that independent startup costs run "approximately $118,000 to $250,000+ to start a spray foam insulation business," with the spray rig alone costing $54,000 to $76,000.

You can start smaller if you buy entry-level gear. Another equipment supplier estimate puts it lower: "Startup costs for a spray foam insulation business typically range from $50,000 to $105,000." That lower range usually leaves out training, marketing, and a cash cushion, so plan for more.

The big difference is what comes with the money. On your own, you buy each piece separately and learn by trial and error. A franchise bundles the equipment, training, and a proven system into one package.

What Your Franchise Investment Covers

That $136,000 to $194,000 is not just a truck and some hoses. It is a full launch package built to get a first-time owner working fast.

Here is what that investment includes:

  • Equipment — the spray rig and gear you need to take jobs from day one.
  • Training — 20 hours of classroom and hands-on training, plus a 1–2 week onboarding at headquarters.
  • Initial inventory — the starting materials to begin serving customers.
  • Launch support — help getting your territory up and running.
  • CRM and tools — a system to track leads and manage customers.
  • Protected territory — an exclusive area so other franchise owners don't compete with you.

There are also no hidden national advertising fees, so the fee structure stays clear. For a full breakdown of the investment and the application, you can download the franchise kit.

Key point: Revenue is not profit. What you keep depends on your material costs, labor, and how busy you stay.

Is a Spray Foam Business Profitable?

Spray foam can be a strong business, but no honest guide promises guaranteed profit. Your results depend on your market, your effort, and how well you run the work.

Industry equipment suppliers report healthy margins for established crews. One supplier's breakdown of spray foam job economics notes that a typical job "equates to a 50% gross profit margin, and many successful contractors achieve net profits of 25% or more." Treat that as an industry estimate, not a promise.

Real franchise data gives another useful benchmark. In a review of Koala Insulation's disclosure, single-territory owners reported franchise revenue where "Single-territory franchisees reported $544,885 on average" in 2024. That figure is gross sales, not take-home profit, so remember your costs come out of it.

Why Demand for Spray Foam Is Growing

Profit potential means little without customers, and demand for spray foam is climbing. Rising energy costs and stricter building efficiency rules are pushing more homeowners and builders toward better insulation.

The market backs this up. Zion Market Research reports the growing spray foam market was "worth around USD 2.87 billion in 2024 and is predicted to grow to around USD 5.47 billion by 2034 with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of roughly 6.8%."

That demand comes from real performance advantages, which you can read about on the why spray foam performs page. It also spans both residential and commercial markets, so you are not tied to a single type of customer.

Do You Need Experience to Start?

No. You do not need prior spray foam, construction, or business experience to get started. The model is built for motivated first-time operators.

Training covers everything that matters:

  • Equipment and application — how to run the rig and spray foam correctly.
  • Sales and operations — how to price jobs, win work, and manage the day-to-day.
  • Marketing and compliance — how to find customers and follow the rules.

You also get direct access to the founder and a ground-floor spot while territories are still open. This is a founder-led business, so the training comes from real field experience.
