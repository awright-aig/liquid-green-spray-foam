Why Allentown & the Lehigh Valley Is a Strong Franchise Market

The Lehigh Valley is one of the most quietly important markets in the Northeast. Comprising Lehigh and Northampton counties, the metro is Pennsylvania's third-largest by population — approximately 680,000 residents across Allentown, Bethlehem, Easton, and a long list of boroughs and townships. Per the U.S. Census Bureau's most recent population estimates, it is one of the fastest-growing metros in the state.

For a spray foam operator, that combination is exactly what you want: enough housing density to keep a crew busy inside a 30-minute drive, and enough new construction on the edge to keep a builder pipeline active. Easton has emerged as a commuter market for NYC and northern New Jersey — buyers who can afford high-performance insulation packages.

Then there is the industrial heritage. The Lehigh Valley was built on steel, cement, brewing, and heavy manufacturing. That DNA has evolved — Air Products, Mack Trucks (now Volvo Group), Crayola, and mid-sized manufacturers still anchor the regional economy — but it left behind a deep bench of older institutional, commercial, and light-industrial buildings that need real envelope work. Lehigh Valley Hospital and St. Luke's drive ongoing healthcare construction. The trade-off is competition — the Valley is a working market with established trades. The Liquid Green system gives a new operator the equipment, the training, and the brand to win on quality and reliability.

Construction Industry & New Build in the Lehigh Valley

PA building permits have run steadily above the long-term statewide average, and the Lehigh Valley has been one of the regions carrying that growth. Per the most recent PA Department of Labor & Industry data, construction employment in the Lehigh Valley workforce investment area remains one of the larger sectors in the regional economy.

New construction skews suburban — single-family subdivisions in Upper Macungie, Lower Macungie, and Forks townships; infill in Bethlehem Township; and a steady pipeline of multifamily in Allentown, Bethlehem, and Easton. Custom homes are common on the perimeter — Saucon Valley, the Slate Belt, and the rolling townships south of Route 22.

The institutional and commercial side is strong. Lehigh Valley Hospital, St. Luke's University Health Network, Lehigh University, Muhlenberg College, Lafayette College, and K-12 school districts run ongoing capital projects. Many of these older buildings have masonry cavity walls, uninsulated roof decks, and additions built across multiple decades — textbook candidates for spray foam. Air Products in Trexlertown and Mack Trucks / Volvo Group in Macungie add an industrial pipeline. Reputation compounds fast.

Energy Efficiency Tailwind in Eastern Pennsylvania

Eastern Pennsylvania gets cold winters and humid summers. Heating-degree days along the Lehigh Valley are comparable to southern New England, and residential electricity rates in PA — set against the PJM Interconnection wholesale market — move with regional fuel prices. PJM is the largest competitive wholesale electricity market in North America, and PA customers are exposed to those swings.

The PA weatherization picture is layered. LIHEAP runs federally through PA's Department of Human Services and helps income-eligible households pay heating bills and fund envelope upgrades. The PA Department of Community and Economic Development (DCED) also funds the Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) for income-eligible homes, which often includes insulation and air sealing.

On the utility side, PPL Electric Utilities and Met-Ed (a FirstEnergy company) serve most of the Lehigh Valley, and both run energy-efficiency programs that include insulation and air-sealing rebates. Those rebates stack with the federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C) and the Residential Clean Energy Credit (25D) for eligible homeowners.

PA's 2021 IECC update raised ceiling and wall R-value targets, so the code floor keeps moving up. Spray foam is the highest-R-value insulation per inch of any common product, and it air-seals at the same time. With PA's housing stock median age around 60 years — older than the national average — the gap between current and code performance is enormous, and that gap is the operator's market. Industry estimates put spray foam demand in the U.S. growing at high single digits year over year.

Local Work, Local Jobs, Hometown Business

This is a hometown business. The franchise operator is the face of the company at the Great Allentown Fair, Musikfest in Bethlehem, the Crayola Experience in Easton, and at every job site. The Lehigh Valley is a community with strong identity.

Most operators start with a small install crew — a lead installer and a helper — and add crew as the job book grows. Crew members are full-time W-2 employees with on-the-job training in spray application, PPE, and customer communication. Hiring locally is a core part of the model: Lehigh Valley jobs, for Lehigh Valley people, on Lehigh Valley homes, schools, and businesses.

Over time, the footprint grows. A successful operator adds a second rig, a second crew, an estimator, and a customer service / office person — real job creation in the neighborhoods where the operator lives, sends their kids to school, and pays property taxes. For operators who want a real industrial base and a real growth story, the Lehigh Valley is a strong fit.

The Lehigh Valley territory is a single protected franchise area covering both Lehigh and Northampton counties in eastern Pennsylvania — including Allentown, Bethlehem, Easton, Emmaus, Macungie, Whitehall, Northampton (borough), Nazareth, and Bangor. No. Most of our franchisees come from outside the trades — sales, military, manufacturing, HVAC, project management.
