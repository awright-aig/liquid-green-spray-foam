What We Install in NJ

Older North Jersey homes with masonry walls

Hoboken, Jersey City, Montclair, Newark, and parts of the older North Jersey suburbs have a lot of solid masonry construction — brick, block, stone, and concrete. These walls were never insulated. Spray foam is the right answer:

  • Apply closed cell to the interior face of the masonry, which adds R-value, controls moisture migration, and stops air leaks through the wall assembly
  • Alternatively, drill-and-inject closed cell into hollow masonry cavity walls (similar to a brownstone retrofit in NYC)
  • Seal rim joists with closed cell — typically the #1 air-leakage location in these homes
  • Insulate attic / roof assemblies with open cell on the floor or closed cell on the underside of the roof deck

Jersey Shore homes

Elevated coastal homes, raised ranches, and vacation properties along the Shore have their own insulation challenges — wind exposure, salt air, moisture, and tight building assemblies. Closed cell is the standard for shore homes because it:

  • Acts as a vapor barrier — critical in moisture-prone shore environments
  • Adds structural rigidity — important for elevated homes exposed to high winds
  • Resists pests (no food value for rodents or insects) — important for seasonal homes that sit empty for months
  • Provides the highest R-value per inch — important where wall thickness is limited

New construction

New-construction subdivisions across Central and South Jersey — Cranbury, Princeton Junction, Robbinsville, Toms River, Jackson, Manalapan — are a growing share of our work. Same system as elsewhere: open or closed cell, sized to the assembly, meeting code with thinner walls than fiberglass would require.

Commercial

Warehouses (especially along the I-95 and NJ Turnpike corridors), retail, light industrial, restaurants, and institutional projects. Closed cell is standard for commercial NJ work. The commercial guide covers the deeper approach.

NJ Codes & Climate

New Jersey homes fall under the NJ Uniform Construction Code, which adopts the 2021 IECC with NJ-specific amendments. NJ is in IECC climate zone 5A (mixed humid) across most of the state, with pockets of 4A in the south and 6A in the northwest highlands. The IECC zone drives minimum R-values, and the right foam (open vs. closed) depends on the assembly and moisture exposure — especially for Shore homes and masonry retrofits in the older North Jersey cities.
