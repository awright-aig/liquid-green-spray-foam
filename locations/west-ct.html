What We Install in This Territory

Historic homes, colonials, capes, and saltboxes

Western CT has one of the densest concentrations of pre-war and early-century housing in the country. Greenwich and Fairfield are full of 1920s-40s colonials; the Litchfield Hills are full of 18th and 19th century capes and farmhouses. These homes were built before modern insulation standards, often with no wall insulation at all, balloon framing, and ice-dam-prone attics.

Spray foam is the right answer for most of them. We typically:

  • Spray the attic floor with open cell (or the underside of the roof deck with closed cell, if you're converting the attic to conditioned space)
  • Seal the rim joists with closed cell — the #1 air-leakage location in old homes
  • Inject closed cell into exterior wall cavities through small holes drilled in the siding or sheathing (preserving original plaster interior walls)
  • Insulate basement walls with closed cell against the fieldstone or poured foundation

For a deeper comparison, see spray foam vs. fiberglass and R-value explained.

Equestrian properties and barns

Litchfield County and northern Fairfield have a lot of equestrian properties — horse barns, indoor riding arenas, tack rooms, and residential portions of equestrian estates. Spray foam is the right answer here because:

  • Closed cell is pest-resistant (no food value for rodents)
  • Spray foam controls moisture and condensation — critical in barn environments
  • The air-sealing cuts heating costs in large unconditioned structures
  • It's fast — a typical riding arena can be sprayed in a day or two

New construction

New-construction subdivisions in towns like Newtown, Brookfield, and Southbury are a growing share of our work. Builders like spray foam because:

  • It passes code with thinner walls (higher R-value per inch than batts)
  • The air-sealing means the house is easier to heat and cool
  • It speeds up the insulation step on the schedule
  • Buyers recognize the value — spray foam homes sell faster and at a premium

Commercial

Less commercial work in this territory than the Hudson Valley, but we still insulate commercial buildings, retail spaces, light industrial, and institutional facilities across Western CT. Same approach as our other territories — see the commercial guide.

Connecticut Codes & Climate

Connecticut homes fall under the Connecticut State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 IECC with state amendments. Climate zones vary across Western CT:

  • Coastal Fairfield County (Greenwich, Stamford, Norwalk) — IECC zone 4A (mixed humid)
  • Inland Fairfield County (Danbury, Ridgefield) — IECC zone 5A
  • Litchfield County — IECC zone 5A in the lower towns, zone 6A in the higher elevations

The coastal humidity is a real consideration — closed cell's vapor-barrier property is a meaningful advantage on exterior walls and rim joists in Greenwich, Stamford, and the shoreline towns. Further inland, both open and closed cell work well. Spray foam safety and open vs. closed cell cover the differences.
