What We Install in This Territory

Our Hudson Valley crews run the full residential and commercial spray foam catalog:

Residential — the bread and butter

  • Attic encapsulation — open cell over the floor deck, or closed cell on the underside of the roof deck. Most common Hudson Valley job. Stops ice dams, kills the stack effect, brings attics inside the conditioned envelope.
  • Rim joists — the #1 air-leakage location in most homes. Closed cell, ~3" thick. Knocks 10-15% off heating bills by itself.
  • Cathedral ceilings and bonus rooms — spray between rafters where conventional batts leave gaps and air leaks.
  • Basement walls — closed cell against poured concrete or block, with a thermal and moisture break. Code-compliant for habitable basement conversions.
  • Crawl spaces — encapsulated with closed cell on the walls, vapor barrier on the floor. Turns a vented crawl into a conditioned one.
  • Whole-home retrofits — exterior wall injection (we open the siding or drill through the sheathing) for older homes without wall insulation.

Commercial

  • Metal building and pole barn insulation (the dominant commercial ask in our territory)
  • Warehouse and agricultural structure insulation
  • New-construction residential subdivisions — we contract directly with builders
  • Commercial retrofits and tenant fit-outs

Local Building Codes & Climate

Hudson Valley homes fall under the New York State Residential Code, which adopts the 2020 IECC. Climate zone 5 (with some 4 and 6 pockets) drives the following minimum R-values for spray foam:

  • Attic — R-49 to R-60 (zone 5); R-60 in zone 6
  • Wood-frame wall — R-20 to R-30 cavity + continuous, depending on zone
  • Floor over unconditioned space — R-30
  • Basement wall — R-15 continuous for habitable basements
  • Crawl space wall — R-15 continuous, plus vapor barrier on the floor

These are the floors. We typically recommend hitting slightly above the code minimum to capture the air-sealing bonus that makes spray foam worth the premium in the first place. R-value explained covers the science.

Is open cell or closed cell better for the Hudson Valley climate?

Both work. Closed cell (R-6 to R-7 per inch) is typical for rim joists, basements, and exterior walls where you want a vapor barrier. Open cell (R-3.5 to R-3.7 per inch) is typical for attics and interior walls. Your assembly and code-required R-value drive the choice.
